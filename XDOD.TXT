QUICK START
===========
LOAD"XDOD.PRG",8      (load/save games also use DEVICE 8)
RUN

Press any key to start the first level.
If no key is pressed, a self-play demo is presented.

You can queue in starting commands during the "PREPARE" scene.
Suggested start:
P R T     (pull torch from inventory to right hand)
U R       (use the torch to light it)
P L SW    (pull sword to left hand)
M         (move one cell)
T A       (turn around)
A L       (attack with sword in left hand)
HINT: You can use sword in right hand if you prefer, 
use "P R SW" and "A R" instead in that case.


INTRODUCTION
============
Dungeons of Daggorath (DoD) is a historic dungeon crawl game.  
Packed to fit into just one 8KB ROM, it became one of the most 
famous games from 1982.  

Featuring...

- Real time player/creature movement and combat
- Time limited torches (15/30/60 minute, and 5 minute dead torches)
- Degraded vision as torches wear out
- A constant heartbeat audio that is your only player-stat indicator
- A "fainting" game mechanic, where overexerting yourself can cause the
  character to faint- and sometimes can't recover!
- Dungeons that are technically randomly generated, but use a selected
  seed so that they always come out arranged the same for each level.
- Excellent use of the CoCo 6-bit DAC audio, interwoven with the gameplay
- Creature audio scales to their distance from the player
- A meaningful REVEAL/INCANT game mechanic to unlock more powerful items
- A 4-row command terminal while also a simulteneous 3D depiction 
  of the game state around the player.

Daggorath was also part of the plot in the Ready Player One 
novel from 2011.

While simplistic in todays terms, Daggorath was groundbreaking
for its time!  Hardly any game since then has reproduced the 
heartbeat and torch fading effects in such an immersive fashion.

For many years Daggorath was exclusive-software to the Tandy Color Computer
system.  The first non-6809 based port wasn't until 2002!  Around this
same year, the prime creator of Daggorath (Douglas Morgan, 
DynaMicro President) deemed the work to be "non-exclusive license"
(provided there is effort to preserve the original form).  The source 
code was publically archived in 2018.
https://github.com/MichaelSpencerJr/DungeonsOfDaggorath


Original Dungeons of Daggorath development team (of DynaMicro in San Diego):

  Douglas J. Morgan
  Phil Landmeier
  Keith S. Kiyohara
  April Landmeier



COMMANDS
========

EXAMINE         E
LOOK            L
  Examine what is in your inventory backpack.  To exit
  the examination view and return to the dungeon view, 
  use the LOOK command.  The currently USED (active) torch
  is displayed in inverse text.  The EXAMINE view also
  shows a listing of what items are dropped in the current cell.
  If a creature enters your current cell while in the
  backpack-view, the word "CREATURE" appears at the top.

MOVE               M
MOVE BACK          M B
  Move forward one dungeon cell or back one cell.
  
MOVE LEFT          M L
MOVE RIGHT         M R
  Sidestep laterally one cell, either left or right.
  You remain facing the same direction.

TURN LEFT          T L
TURN RIGHT         T R
TURN AROUND        T A 
  Turn left, right, or 180-degree within the current cell.

CLIMB DOWN         C D
CLIMB UP           C U
  Climb down (through hole or with a ladder).  If a ladder is 
  present, you can climb back up (but caution in doing so!).

ATTACK LEFT        A L
ATTACK RIGHT       A R 
  Attacking using whatever item is in your left or right
  hand.  You can even attack empty-handed.  Normally you
  attack with sword, but can also use shield, rings, or
  essentially any other item.  They'll do damage proportional
  to their type.  Attacking with a strong weapon while
  you're still weak will cause exertion (and eventually
  you can faint).
  
GET LEFT <item>    G L <item>
GET RIGHT <item>   G R <item>
  If the corresponding hand is empty, this command picks
  items up off the ground.  Items are SCROLL, FLASK,
  SHIELD, SWORD, RING, TORCH.  The parser also allows
  specifying revealed items.  For example, if there are
  several types of torches, you can do "G R LUNAR T"
  to pick up a Lunar type.
  
PULL LEFT <item>   P L <item>
PULL RIGHT <item>  P R <item>
  If the corresponding hand is empty, this command pulls
  the specified item out of the backpack-inventory.
  For example, the starter move is typically
  PULL RIGHT TORCH   (P R T)
  USE RIGHT          (U R)
  Like the GET command, the parser supports also specific
  item types.  For example, "P R DEAD T" would pull 
  a DEAD torch out of the inventory.

DROP LEFT          D L
DROP RIGHT         D R
  Drop whatever is in your left or right hand onto the
  floor of the current cell.  Such items will persist,
  unless a creature picks it up.  Generally you drop
  dead torches or weaker weapons.  Carrying too much
  will increase exertion while moving.

USE LEFT           U L
USE RIGHT          U R 
  Make use of the item that is either in your left or
  right hand.  You use a torch, scroll, flask.
  
REVEAL LEFT        R L
REVEAL RIGHT       R R
  Certain powerful items can't be used effectively until 
  they have been revealed.  This includes stronger magical 
  torches, rings, scrolls- but also weapons like sword and
  shield.  If you can't reveal an item, then you're likely
  not ready to make use of it yet.  One exception is FLASK,
  you can risk those at any time.

INCANT <word>      I <word>
  Rings must be incanted before their full power can be
  used in an attack.  Revealing a ring gives a hint on
  what its incantation word might be.  Once incanted,
  a ring only has three shots before returning to a normal
  Gold Ring.  The incantation word must be typed in full.

ZSAVE              SAVE TO DEFAULT XDOD.SAV FILE
ZSAVE <slot>       SAVE TO XDOD-<slot>.SAV (slot can be 1 to 8 digits)
ZSAVE <slot> F     SAME AS ABOVE, BUT NO REPLICA TAPE-AUDIO

ZLOAD              LOAD USING DEFAULT XDOD.SAV FILE
ZLOAD <slot>       LOAD USING XDOD-<slot>.SAV
ZLOAD <slot> F     SAME AS ABOVE, BUT NO REPLICA TAPE-AUDIO

  NOTE: The ZSAVE and ZLOAD assume use of DEVICE 8 and CWD.

COLOR 0            BLACK AND WHITE (same as just "COLOR")
COLOR 1            FAUX COCO1 STYLE ARTEFACTING RED/BLUE
COLOR 2            "CRISP" COCO2 STYLE
COLOR 3            "CRISP" COCO3 RGB STYLE (BLUE/YELLOW)
  NOTE: Repeating "COLOR 1", 2, or 3 will "invert" the colors of that mode.

BUG
  Toggles the "shield bug" on or off.  "ON" is the original
  authentic way the game was played.


HINTS
=====
The original Daggorath manual is intentionally somewhat-vague,
in a "no spoilers" fashion.  This builds up the lore and mystery
of the game.

Personally, I imagined a back story prequel where the villagers
had invaded the Wizards lands -- thus explaining why he was so
bitter.  Intersting stories tend to build up backstories on
villains.  e.g. 1992 Batman Return movie with The Penguin 
comes to mind.

I'll give a few hints that the original manual didn't:

- If you move too much while carrying a lot of things,
  the heartrate goes up faster.  So move slow and steady.
- Banging into walls can cause you to faint!
- The creatures do pick up items.  Study on this, since
  you can use this to your advantage.  But there are two 
  types of creatures that have an exception to this rule,
  the Wizard being obvious since he doesn't care about items.
- With the starter PINE torch, you can't see magical
  doorways (they appear triangular with the better torches).
  But you can still walk through them- this is why sometimes
  it appears you can walk through walls.


WHAT IS XDOD (background)
============
XDOD is a Commander X16 port of the original source code as archived here:
https://github.com/MichaelSpencerJr/DungeonsOfDaggorath

The Commander X16 is an 8MHz 65C02 based system, but also has
specialized video (and PCM audio) provided by the VERA daughter board.

I played Daggorath originally on a 64KB Tandy Color Computer II,
with the S-video adapted to the RF-input of a television.
I did finally beat the game on my own, in 1991, after many years
of pondering the ring incantations.  

During COVID, I happened to receive an original silver-colored
16KB Tandy Color Computer.  Around 2022, I ended up recording a full
play-thru of the game.  That recording is available here:
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NotVeSUw4o4
(see the Description of that video for links to the ZSAVE'd games
created during that play-thru).
It was also in this year that I learned that the original Daggorath ROM 
plays slightly differently on the CoCo3 (mainly because of the
slightly different GIME graphics hardware it had).

XDOD development began Sunday November 8th, 2026, 
using GPT 6.1 Desktop, with the prompted intention of being
produced to run on the X16 and making use of the ca65 assembler.
The resulting full-playable release was available 2-days later.
This started as a personal experiment, since I hadn't used 
GPT Codex before.

This manual, however, was completely written manually - 
except for the chart at the very end.


KNOWN DIFFERENCES ("enhancements")
=================
The primary goal was to replicate the original Daggorath experience,
and to match all of its original timing (as played on the original
16KB Color Computer when the game was first available) and game
mechanics to the extent possible.

But during the development, I did take a few liberties:

- a "COLOR" command is added to choose between several different
  visual styles.  Since most folks don't use CRTs anymore,
  the color-artifacting of the original is lost on modern hardware.
  So COLOR 1 is an attempt to replicate that style.
  COLOR 3 is how I believe CoCo3 RGB would visualize the game.
  COLOR 0 is a purely black and white style, that may end up
  having artifacts if you do use a certain CRT.
  
- With the last few bytes of X16 LoRAM available, I decided to
  go ahead and add a "BUG" command that toggles whether the
  original "shield bug" is present.  By default, the BUG is
  ON/ENABLED (since that's authentic to the original).
  Typing BUG will toggle this OFF and fix the original bug.
  But what's interesting in HOW the bug fix is applied:
  it uses "self modifying code" so that there is no runtime
  overhead in checking the bug-presence state.

- If you are defeated, the original game would wait for a key at
  the death scene.  For XDOD, I gave this about an 8 second idle before
  returning to the main screen.  This way at things like VCF's,
  the game can cycle back to the self-play demo mode more easily.
  
- ZSAVE/ZLOAD happens instantly on the X16.  So by default, I
  intentionally slow it down and play a "faux" tape-data noise
  during the save or load action. It's semi-authentic in that the
  audio tones will vary with the data involved.  The original
  game took about 30 seconds to save.  For XDOD, I set this
  audio-replica of saving to 3 seconds.   You can also have
  mutliple saved games, and you can skip the faux-tape-noise
  by adding "F" to the end of the ZSAVE/ZLOAD command (F for FAST).
  This was just a audio hommage to what we experienced originally
  in playing Daggorath.
  NOTE: In 1986, a DaggorPatch floated around that patched the
  original binary and allowed disk-based saved games (and other
  features like a print-screen capture).

- The self-play demo is mostly accurate to the original, there
  are a couple minor differences.  I tried my best to get the 
  timing aligned.  That they packed this auto-self-play into 
  the 8KB ROM is still amazing to me.    But the way it works 
  means it is very sensitive to the CPU speed (whether real 
  or simulated).
  

SHIELD BUG: WHAT IS IT?
=======================

The brief answer: 
  Apparent reversal of Leather and Bronze shield defenses,
  relative to their Physical vs Magical categories.

In the original Dungeons of Daggorath, the Leather and Bronze shields 
reduce magical damage and provide no protection against physical damage. 
That makes them ineffective against early enemies such as spiders and vipers. 
This is what players call the shield bug.
(and this has been identified in the original cartridge ROM binary disassembly)

The fix swaps those two effects. Leather reduces physical damage by 
about 16%, and Bronze by 25%. Both give up their magical protection. 
Mithril is unaffected because it already halves both types of damage. 
Shields change how much damage a landed hit deals, while the chance of 
being hit stays the same.

XDOD starts with the original behavior. Type BUG once to apply the fix 
(SHIELD BUG OFF) and again to restore the original (SHIELD BUG ON).
The BUG state isn't reset during death, replay of the self-demo, or 
a ZLOAD or any other means- it stays to whatever you last set it,
until you restart the system or reload the PRG.


I asked GPT to very explicitly explain this bug, in the form of a chart.
The BUG is related to a multiplier that gets applied - so the fix isn't
changing table-values per se, but correcting which category has which
multiplier (Mithril technically has the same bug, but since its the same
multiplier for both Physical and Magical, the bug doesn't impact it).
